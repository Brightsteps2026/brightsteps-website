Who We Are: Grade 3 and 4 Celebrate Themselves

On Thursday, October 15, our Grade 3 and 4 students celebrated the end of their Who We Are unit of inquiry by sharing who they are with their classmates.

Each student created a self portrait and wrote about themselves, using the "What Makes Me Proud" writing guide to plan and improve their paragraphs. The posters are full of personality: favorite sports and animals, families and friends, and the words students chose to describe themselves, from smart and brave to kind and friendly.

Presenting their work helped our learners practice speaking with confidence, listening to one another, and appreciating what makes each person unique.

Well done, Grade 3 and 4. We are proud of you.

Beyond Flags and Accents: What Makes a School Truly International?

At BrightSteps, we often hear the question, what exactly makes a school international? Sure, you might imagine colorful flags in the hallways and students speaking different accents, and yes, you will find those here. But being truly international is about much more, it is about the way we think, learn, and grow together.

An international school does not just have students from different countries, it teaches children how to think like global citizens. English is our main language of instruction, with French as a strong supporting language, so children learn to express themselves confidently in both, building not just fluency but connection.

Our approach goes beyond academics. From tending plants in the school garden to exploring robotics, from performing on stage to solving real world challenges, we nurture confidence, creativity, and character.

International School or Traditional School: What Is the Difference?

Choosing a school is an important decision for every family. While both international and traditional schools support children's education, their approaches can be different.

Traditional schools generally follow a national curriculum and teach primarily in the country's official language. Learning may focus more on textbooks, memorisation and examinations.

International schools often use English as the main language of instruction and provide a broader global perspective. Students are encouraged to ask questions, explore ideas, collaborate and apply what they learn to real-life situations.

International education also develops skills such as:

  • Critical thinking
  • Creativity
  • Communication
  • Independence
  • Cultural awareness
  • Collaboration

This does not mean that one type of school is right for every child. Parents should consider the curriculum, teaching approach, language of instruction, class size and support available to students.

At BrightSteps International School, we provide an English-medium, inquiry-based education in a caring environment. We help students build strong academic foundations while developing the confidence, curiosity and character they need for the future.
